## Headline: TN Government Prepares Pongal Rollout for Free LPG Refills While Political Crosscurrents Intensify Across Delta and Courts

## The Big Picture
Tamil Nadu's political landscape is currently dominated by major administrative rollouts, high-stakes judicial interventions, and intensifying agrarian friction. Chief Minister C. Joseph Vijay’s administration is accelerating preparations to shortlist households for the flagship 'Annapurani Super 6' welfare scheme, which promises three free domestic LPG cylinder refills annually starting from the upcoming Pongal festival. Funded through direct bank transfers, the initiative aims to benefit up to 1.3 million families across the state, serving as a cornerstone of the ruling Tamilaga Vettri Kazhagam’s governance agenda.

Simultaneously, the state is grappling with severe agricultural distress in the Cauvery delta districts, prompting sharp political confrontations. Leader of the Opposition Udhayanidhi Stalin has demanded immediate, transparent crop loss surveys and direct drought relief, blaming the ruling dispensation's water management for failing the Kuruvai cultivation. These agrarian anxieties intersect with broader communal and legal flashpoints, notably the ongoing controversy over the Thirupparankundram deepathoon ruling, where the state government is actively consulting legal experts regarding a potential Supreme Court review petition against an interim order permitting the lighting of the lamp.

## Assembly, Courts, and Policy
- **Madras High Court Rules Inter-Faith Hindu-Christian Marriages Cannot Validate Under Hindu Marriage Act:** In a significant legal pronouncement, the Madras High Court ruled that a marriage contracted between a Hindu and a Christian cannot be legally validated through registration under the Hindu Marriage Act. The bench emphasized that statutory provisions governing personal law frameworks strictly require parties to adhere to the Special Marriage Act when formalizing inter-faith unions. The ruling stems from a writ petition challenging administrative registration practices across sub-registrar offices in the state. Legal experts note this precedent will significantly impact family law jurisprudence and civil registration procedures throughout Tamil Nadu, reinforcing the primacy of secular legislative statutes in inter-faith matrimonial disputes.
