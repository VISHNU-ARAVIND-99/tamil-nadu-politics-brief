## Headline: Political Shockwaves in Tamil Nadu as Major Drug Busts Spark Opposition Outcry, High-Profile Raids, and Legal Battles Over Water Rights

## The Big Picture
The Tamil Nadu political landscape is witnessing severe turbulence, dominated by fierce clashes between the ruling administration and major opposition parties over law enforcement, welfare distribution, and inter-state water disputes. The seizure of ₹700 crore worth of heroin in Chennai by the Directorate of Revenue Intelligence has triggered relentless attacks from both the DMK and AIADMK, who accuse the government of severe security lapses and deteriorating law and order. Concurrently, political friction has intensified over welfare schemes, with the opposition aggressively challenging reports that thousands of beneficiaries have been dropped from the Kalaignar Magalir Urimai Thogai initiative.

Adding to the governance challenges, anti-corruption crackdowns have taken center stage following police raids on former DMK minister P. Moorthi's residence over alleged quarrying irregularities causing a ₹5.39 crore loss to the state exchequer. DMK President M.K. Stalin has vociferously condemned these actions as politically motivated diversionary tactics meant to obscure the central agencies' massive drug busts. Meanwhile, inter-state tensions have peaked following former Prime Minister H.D. Deve Gowda's letter advocating for Karnataka's Mekedatu dam project, prompting unified condemnation from Tamil Nadu farmers and political leaders across party lines, even as the Supreme Court delivered a critical blow by dismissing the state's plea for an additional 11 TMC of Cauvery water.

## Government and Administration
- **30,000 Beneficiaries Removed from Magalir Urimai Scheme; Government Denies 50% Reduction Claims:** State officials confirmed that approximately 30,000 beneficiaries have been systematically removed from the Kalaignar Magalir Urimai Thogai Thittal over the past six months following administrative reviews and the dropping of the 'Kalaignar' prefix. Authorities clarified that these removals were strictly executed on grounds of recipient mortality and significantly improved household financial status, such as securing government employment or becoming income tax payers. The clarification came in response to sharp accusations from BJP State President Nainar Nagenthran, who alleged that the TVK-led government had betrayed women by halting monthly ₹1,000 assistance to more than 50% of the 16.94 lakh beneficiaries added during the second phase in December 2025. While the government strongly rejected the opposition's claims of a mass reduction from the baseline 1.31 crore recipients, the controversy has intensified scrutiny over welfare delivery mechanisms. Concurrently, political circles reacted to the discontinuation of the Justice Kurian Joseph-chaired High-Level Committee on Union–State Relations, with the administration defending the move by citing a mandate overlap with the reconstituted Tamil Nadu State Law Commission. ## Assembly, Courts, and Policy
- **Supreme Court Rejects Tamil Nadu's Plea for 11 TMC Cauvery Water; High Court Defers Fake Book Case:** In a major legal setback for the state's agricultural sector, the Supreme Court officially dismissed Tamil Nadu's interim application seeking an additional 11 TMC of Cauvery water from neighboring Karnataka. The apex court's ruling leaves delta farmers facing severe irrigation deficits during the ongoing cropping season, heightening agrarian distress across the Cauvery basin districts. Concurrently, the Madras High Court deferred its hearing on a high-profile petition filed by DMK legal wing joint secretary I. Paranthamen, which sought a Directorate of Vigilance and Anti-Corruption (DVAC) probe into alleged irregularities in book procurement for the Periyar Arivulagam in Coimbatore. The presiding judge ruled that the court would await the final determination of a pending division bench order at Madurai concerning the alleged supply of AI-generated books with fictitious authors and unverifiable ISBN numbers. Additionally, left-wing parties and the PMK intensified pressure on the state government to immediately file an appeal before the Supreme Court against a recent Madras High Court order that struck down Section 3A, a legal provision banning the inter-state transport of minerals.
