## The Big Picture
Tamil Nadu's political landscape faces intense legal and administrative scrutiny as the Madras High Court delivers a series of landmark judgments, including the striking down of Rule 3-A which restricted the inter-state transport of legally mined minerals. These developments intersect with shifting political alignments following Congress's exit from the DMK alliance, leading to notable diplomatic chilliness between former allies while opposition parties navigate controversies surrounding public demonstrations and digital commentary.

## Assembly, Courts, and Policy
- **Madras High Court Strikes Down Rule 3-A, Ruling Tamil Nadu Cannot Ban Inter-State Mineral Transport:** In a major victory for industrial associations and mining operators, the Madras High Court formally struck down Rule 3-A of the Tamil Nadu Minor Mineral Concession Rules, which previously prohibited the transport of legally mined minerals to neighboring states like Karnataka and Kerala. The division bench ruled that the state government lacks the legislative competence to impose absolute bans on inter-state commerce of lawfully extracted minerals, noting that such restrictions infringe upon constitutional provisions regarding trade and commerce. Legal counsels for the petitioners argued that the blanket prohibition had crippled regional supply chains and caused severe financial losses to licensed quarry owners. The court's judgment mandates immediate administrative compliance, compelling state authorities to overhaul mineral transit regulations and re-issue permits without territorial discrimination. - **Publishers Allege Policy Violations by DMK Government in Modern Library Book Procurements:** Independent publishing houses have raised serious allegations against the previous DMK administration, accusing authorities of violating their own 15-page book procurement policy during purchases for modern libraries in Madurai and Coimbatore. Introduced in 2024 to foster contemporary literature and modernize institutional libraries, the policy mandates strict transparency and objective selection criteria. Publishers claimed that officials bypassed established guidelines to favor specific literary works, prompting formal demands for immediate policy amendments to guarantee absolute accountability in future government acquisitions. Administrative spokespersons have yet to issue a detailed rebuttal regarding the alleged procedural lapses in the library procurement tenders.
