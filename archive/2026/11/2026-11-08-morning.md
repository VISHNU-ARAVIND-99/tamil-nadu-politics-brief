## Headline: Tamil Nadu Political Upheaval: DMK and TVK Clash Over Periyar Library Book Scandal and Centre-State Panel Dissolution

## The Big Picture
Tamil Nadu's political landscape witnessed intense friction as governance controversies escalated across multiple fronts, dominated by allegations of fraudulent book procurements and high-stakes administrative U-turns. The ruling TVK government, led by Chief Minister C. Joseph Vijay, faced intense opposition fire after dissolving the Kurian Joseph-headed high-level committee on Union-State relations, a move branded by the DMK as the complete burial of state autonomy to appease the Union BJP administration. Simultaneously, a multi-crore controversy erupted over alleged AI-generated and fake book procurements for the Periyar Arivulagam in Coimbatore and public libraries across Chennai, Madurai, Cuddalore, and Tirunelveli, prompting both the ruling dispensation and the opposition DMK to cross swords at the Directorate of Vigilance and Anti-Corruption (DVAC).

Policy adjustments also dominated the governance discourse as the state administration expanded its agricultural relief framework, scrapping the tiered system to offer a Rs 75,000 crop loan waiver to 13.25 lakh eligible farmers while allocating Rs 2,842.774 crore for the 2026-27 fiscal year, though farmer associations maintained their demand for an unconditional total write-off. Meanwhile, geopolitical concerns resurfaced regarding inter-state water disputes as Chief Minister Vijay wrote to Prime Minister Narendra Modi protesting Union official site inspections for Karnataka's proposed Mekedatu dam project. These compounding developments highlight a polarizing administrative climate where opposition parties are aggressively challenging executive policy decisions ahead of upcoming legislative sessions and court proceedings.

## Government and Administration
- **Tamil Nadu Government Drops Tiered System for Rs 75,000 Crop Loan Waiver, Allocates Rs 2,842 Crore:** In a major policy revision addressing agrarian distress, the Tamil Nadu government has extended a crop loan waiver of up to Rs 75,000 to every eligible farmer who borrowed from cooperative societies between May 1, 2025, and March 31, 2026, completely removing the previous tiered system that capped maximum relief at Rs 35,000. The revised scheme is projected to directly benefit approximately 13.25 lakh farmers across the state, with the administration sanctioning an additional Rs 2,842.774 crore, bringing total allocations to Rs 6,220 crore. Despite the expansion, farmers' associations, including the Tamil Nadu Vivasayigal Sangam (CPI), expressed dissatisfaction, reiterating their demand for a complete and unconditional loan waiver in fulfillment of election promises. Simultaneously, Chief Minister C. Joseph Vijay wrote to Prime Minister Narendra Modi urging the Centre to halt statutory approvals for Karnataka's proposed Mekedatu dam project without co-basin state consent. ## Assembly, Courts, and Policy
- **Madras High Court Refuses to Reject Election Petition Against TVK MLA Over Child Campaigning Violations:** The Madras High Court has dismissed a discharge petition filed against an election challenge targeting TVK MLA Vijayakumar alias Navalapattu Viji, who won the Thiruverumbur Assembly constituency in the 2026 elections. Former Minister and DMK leader Anbil Mahesh Poyyamozhi had filed the initial election petition alleging that the victorious legislator engaged children during his campaign, violating Election Commission of India guidelines. Justice Kumaresh Babu ruled that the ECI's instructions against deploying minors in political campaigns carry statutory force, meaning the factual veracity and legal consequences of the allegations must be thoroughly tested during a full judicial trial. The court's refusal to summarily dismiss the petition ensures that the high-profile electoral dispute will proceed to active litigation, setting a crucial legal precedent regarding campaign code enforcement in Tamil Nadu.
