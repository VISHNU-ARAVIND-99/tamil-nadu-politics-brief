## Headline: DMK Backs Centre's Delimitation Bill While Supreme Court Slams TVK Government Over Thirupparankundram Security Order

## The Big Picture
Tamil Nadu's political landscape faces intense realignment following major policy and judicial developments. The DMK's decision to support the Union government's revised delimitation bill—citing negotiated safeguards that keep Tamil Nadu's representation share at 7.21%—has triggered friction within the INDIA bloc, drawing sharp criticism from the VCK, Left parties, and the Congress. Meanwhile, the ruling TVK administration is grappling with severe institutional crises, highlighted by the Supreme Court transferring the security of the Thirupparankundram hill area to the CRPF and issuing contempt notices to top state bureaucrats.

 Concurrently, state administration has been shaken by the arrest of Kallakurichi TVK MLA C. Arul Vignesh on charges of harassing a senior woman IAS officer, drawing heavy condemnation from opposition leaders who point to administrative failures. These simultaneous flashpoints over federal policy, judicial reprimands, and executive accountability underscore a volatile phase in Tamil Nadu governance, altering political equations between regional parties and national players ahead of upcoming parliamentary sessions.

## Government and Administration
- **Madras High Court Upholds O. Panneerselvam's Bodinayakanur Election Victory:** The Madras High Court issued a significant verdict dismissing an election petition filed by P. Milany, which had challenged the assembly election victory of former Chief Minister and DMK MLA O. Panneerselvam from the Bodinayakanur constituency. Panneerselvam, who secured the seat with a 6,805-vote margin during the recent assembly elections won by the ruling TVK, successfully argued for the rejection of the challenge. Simultaneously, the court ruled that separate election petitions targeting other legislators warrant full-fledged judicial trials. Legal experts view the dismissal as a stabilization of legislative mandates for senior figures navigating complex post-election litigations across various judicial benches. ## Political Parties and Opposition
- **INDIA Bloc Fractures as VCK and Left Parties Condemn DMK's Stance on Delimitation:** The decision by the DMK to support the Union Cabinet's delimitation-linked constitutional amendment bill has severely strained relations within the state's opposition alliance. VCK founder Thol Thirumavalavan publicly appealed to the DMK to reconsider its alignment, arguing that the BJP-led Union government's overarching design undermines federalism and state legislative strength. Left parties echoed these warnings, while Congress MP Manickam Tagore alleged a tactical surrender by regional parties for short-term political convenience. Political observers note that while the DMK insists its three-year advocacy successfully safeguarded Tamil Nadu's proportional allocation at 7.21%, the widening rift with national and regional allies signals potential long-term realignments in state opposition dynamics ahead of parliamentary floor tests. ([The Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/chennai/vck-left-parties-urge-dmk-to-oppose-delimitation-bill-tagore-alleges-u-turn/articleshow/136231514.cms))

## Assembly, Courts, and Policy
- **Supreme Court Contempt Notices and CRPF Deployment Redefine State Jurisdiction:** The Supreme Court's decisive intervention in the Thirupparankundram Deepathoon controversy has created deep ripples across legislative and administrative corridors. By issuing contempt notices to the Chief Secretary, DGP, and HR&CE Department officials alongside transferring temple hill security to the CRPF, the apex court has cast direct doubt on the executive competence of the TVK government. AIADMK general secretary Edappadi K. Palaniswami and BJP leader Nainar Nagenthran asserted that the judicial strictures demonstrate a total collapse of administrative machinery and the protection of religious traditions. Legal analysts emphasize that invoking central paramilitary forces for a state temple dispute sets a rare precedent, intensifying debates over federal overreach and state administrative autonomy in temple management. ([The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/tamil-nadu/nagenthran-annamalai-welcome-sc-order-on-thirupparankundram-issue/article71751705.cms))
