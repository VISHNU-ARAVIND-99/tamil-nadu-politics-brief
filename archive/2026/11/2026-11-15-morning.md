## Headline: Civil Servants Urge Revival of Union-State Panel as Political Tensions Rise Over Administrative Scandals and High-Level Probes

## The Big Picture
The political landscape of Tamil Nadu is witnessing heightened friction as civil society groups, opposition parties, and administrative bodies clash over governance transparency and federal rights. A collective of over 70 retired IAS and IPS officers, operating under the banner of the Constitutional Conduct Group, has formally appealed to Chief Minister C. Joseph Vijay to reinstate the original mandate of the High-Level Committee on Union-State Relations and restore its Part I report online. This civil service intervention underscores ongoing anxieties surrounding federal autonomy and administrative accountability across the state.

Concurrently, the state administration is grappling with multiple internal controversies, most notably the suspension of five senior officials from the Public Libraries Department following a Madras High Court directive concerning questionable book procurements involving AI-generated works and fictitious authors. Political sparring has intensified across party lines, with opposition leaders targeting the ruling dispensation over law and order, inflation metrics, and high-profile inquiries. As Chief Minister Vijay prepares for national engagements in New Delhi, including participation in senior political events, the domestic arena remains charged with protests, judicial petitions, and aggressive posturing from both the AIADMK and various regional political formations.

## Government and Administration
- **Tamil Nadu Government Restores Kadapakkam Lake Under ₹58.31-Crore Eco-Infrastructure Project:** Chief Minister C. Joseph Vijay is scheduled to inaugurate the fully restored Kadapakkam Lake following a massive ₹58.31-crore infrastructure overhaul designed to maximize urban floodwater storage capacity and rejuvenate local biodiversity. The comprehensive project includes deep desilting, embankment reinforcement, and the creation of dedicated ecological zones to support migratory bird populations and indigenous aquatic species. Urban planners have lauded the initiative as a vital step toward mitigating Chennai's recurrent monsoon inundation challenges through decentralized water-body management. Environmental ministries coordinated closely with municipal engineers to ensure minimal disruption to surrounding habitats while maximizing groundwater recharge rates across the drought-prone suburban belt. Local civic associations welcomed the inauguration, noting that similar hydrological revamps are urgently required across neighboring districts to safeguard urban settlements from future climate extremes. ## Assembly, Courts, and Policy
- **PMK Files Public Interest Litigation in Madras High Court Seeking Official Status for Tamil in Judiciary:** The Pattali Makkal Katchi (PMK), through party general secretary Vadivel Ravanan, has moved the Madras High Court with a public interest litigation petition compelling the state government to forward historical legislative resolutions to the Union government. The legal action demands that the TVK administration immediately transmit unanimous Assembly resolutions passed in 2006 and 2026 seeking the designation of Tamil as the official principal language of the Madras High Court. The petitioner argued that securing presidential assent and formal central concurrence is vital for enabling rural litigants and advocates from Backward Classes and Most Backward Classes to comprehend judicial proceedings fully. Legal experts anticipate that the High Court will issue notices to both state and union law departments to clarify the administrative delays surrounding the long-pending linguistic transition. The petition has reignited broader political debates regarding regional language rights and the accessibility of the judicial apparatus to common citizens.
