## Headline: Karnataka Pushes Mekedatu Project as Tamil Nadu Opposes, Sparking Inter-State Tensions

## The Big Picture
The political and administrative landscape of Tamil Nadu is witnessing heightened friction across multiple fronts, dominated by inter-state water disputes and internal party realignments. Karnataka's renewed push for the Mekedatu balancing reservoir has drawn sharp opposition from state leadership, with Chief Minister C Joseph Vijay formally writing to Prime Minister Narendra Modi to stall the venture. Simultaneously, administrative machinery faces scrutiny following controversies surrounding library book procurement and subsequent bureaucratic reshuffles, alongside major infrastructure rollouts such as the newly formed Tamil Nadu Infrastructure and Construction Corporation.

Simultaneously, grassroots political realignments are reshaping the state's party dynamics, highlighted by cross-party defections and consequential legal interventions from the AIADMK and the Election Commission of India. As urban transit hubs grapple with holiday congestion and the Meteorological Department issues heavy rainfall warnings across multiple southern districts, governance structures are under intense public and legislative pressure to balance infrastructural expansion with environmental sustainability and administrative transparency.

## Government and Administration
- **Tamil Nadu Government Issues Order Establishing Infrastructure and Construction Corporation for Major Projects:** The Tamil Nadu government has officially issued a government order establishing the Tamil Nadu Infrastructure and Construction Corporation to execute public building projects exceeding Rs. 200 crore. According to the official notification, the newly formed corporate body will also commercially utilize unused government lands across the state to generate independent public revenue. Furthermore, the corporation is authorized to participate in competitive national and international commercial tenders for private sector construction contracts. Administration officials emphasize that this entity will streamline large-scale capital expenditure, though opposition factions have demanded legislative oversight regarding its financial autonomy and transparency. ## Political Parties and Opposition
- **AIADMK Files Deffection Speaker Notice Against Defectors Joining TVK as Vaiko Bans Banners:** The AIADMK has formally submitted a disqualification notice to the Tamil Nadu Legislative Assembly Speaker against four former members who defected to the Tamizhaga Vetri Kazhagam (TVK). The move signals intensifying political competition as emerging formations draw cadres from traditional Dravidian parties ahead of upcoming legislative cycles. Concurrently, MDMK General Secretary Vaiko issued a strict internal directive declaring that he will boycott any party or personal event where large roadside banners are erected in violation of civic regulations. Vaiko emphasized that despite widespread usage by politicians, businesses, and religious bodies, unauthorized banners create public hazards that the party can no longer endorse. ## District-Level Developments
- **Clambakkam Bus Station Faces Two-Wheeler Parking Chaos and NGT Intervenes in Tirunelveli Pollution:** Commuters at the newly developed Clambakkam bus terminus near Chennai experienced severe disruptions as the two-wheeler parking facility reached maximum capacity ahead of the multi-day holiday rush, leading to heated arguments with transport staff and police. Passengers protested the reallocation of designated parking spaces for special festive bus fleets, forcing travelers to seek alternative arrangements. Meanwhile, the National Green Tribunal's Principal Bench issued formal notices to the Tirunelveli District Collector and five other authorities over alarming threats to endemic fish species in the Tamirabharani River due to industrial pollution and habitat degradation. Administrative authorities have been directed to submit comprehensive action taken reports regarding river conservation within stipulated deadlines.
